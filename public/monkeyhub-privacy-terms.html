Privacy Policy

MonkeyHub is a messaging app for families. This policy explains, in plain English, exactly what we collect, where it goes, how long we keep it, and what happens when you delete your account. We have written it to be accurate rather than reassuring, because a privacy policy that overstates what an app protects is worse than useless.

The short version:

  • We do not run ads. We do not sell or rent your data. There is no analytics SDK, no attribution SDK and no crash-reporting SDK in this app — none.
  • MonkeyHub runs on servers the developer rents and administers directly, not on a third-party chat platform.
  • Your messages are NOT end-to-end encrypted. They are stored on our server in a form the operator can read. Please read that section before you decide whether MonkeyHub is right for your family.
  • Your data reaches other parties in a few specific places: our push provider, the network provider that carries all our traffic, sign-in with Apple or Google, and — from your own device rather than our server — call setup and link previews. Every one of them is named below.
  • You can delete your account from inside the app, or from the web. Almost everything is erased. A short, specific list of things is kept, and we say exactly what.

MonkeyHub is a small, independently run app. There is no data protection officer, because we are not required to appoint one. We have not appointed a representative in the EU or the UK. Questions go straight to the person who writes the code, at info@walhallaa.com.

MonkeyHub's servers are self-hosted: a Convex database running on a virtual machine rented from Oracle Cloud and administered by the developer, reached through a Cloudflare Tunnel and a Caddy web server. Voice and video calls that cannot connect directly are relayed through our own coturn server on a second rented machine. We do not use a hosted chat provider, and no third-party product sits between you and your conversations except where this policy says so.

Important: your messages are NOT end-to-end encrypted

We want to be completely clear about this, because many messaging apps work differently and you may reasonably assume MonkeyHub does too.

Message text, photos, videos, voice notes and documents are stored on our server in readable form. The operator of MonkeyHub can technically read them. There is no end-to-end encryption. There is no zero-knowledge design. Nothing in the app encrypts your messages with a key only you hold.

A few specific consequences you should understand:

  • The 12-word recovery phrase you are shown when you create an account does not encrypt anything and does not unlock anything. It is not a wallet seed phrase, despite looking like one. Its only function is to let you reset a forgotten password. If you lose it, you lose the ability to reset your password — you do not lose access to encrypted data, because there is none.
  • Because messages are readable on the server, they are also readable in the places messages get copied to: the notification we hand to our push provider, the message preview on a chat's row in your list, and the quoted excerpt shown when someone replies to you.
  • Anyone with administrative access to the server database could read conversations. Today that is the developer. We do not read your messages as a matter of routine, and we do not scan, mine, profile or train anything on them. But we are telling you that we could, because that is the honest description of the system.

We encrypt data in transit (HTTPS/TLS). Whether the underlying disks on our host are encrypted at rest depends on the cloud provider's volume configuration, and we do not currently make a claim about it. Neither transit encryption nor disk encryption is the same as end-to-end encryption. Please do not treat MonkeyHub as a confidential channel for anything that would harm you or your child if it were read.

What we do not do

These are commitments, not marketing:

  • No advertising. There is no ad SDK anywhere in the app, and no advertising identifier is collected. On Android, MonkeyHub does not request the advertising ID permission. On iOS, MonkeyHub never asks for tracking permission and cannot access the IDFA.
  • No analytics or product-tracking SDK. No Google Analytics, Firebase Analytics, Amplitude, Mixpanel, Segment, PostHog, or anything comparable.
  • No crash-reporting SDK. No Crashlytics, Sentry, Bugsnag or similar. When the app crashes, we do not find out unless you tell us.
  • No attribution or install-tracking SDK. No AppsFlyer, Adjust, Branch, or Facebook SDK.
  • We do not sell, rent, licence or trade personal data. Not to advertisers, not to data brokers, not to anyone.
  • We do not build profiles of you and we do not use your messages, photos or voice notes to train machine-learning models.
  • We do not collect location. MonkeyHub requests no location permission on any platform and holds no GPS or coarse-location data. (Call setup does briefly reveal IP addresses — see "Calls" and "Who else receives your data".)
  • We do not read your contacts. MonkeyHub requests no contacts permission and never uploads an address book.
  • We do not download fonts from Google. Every typeface the app uses is bundled inside it, and font fetching over the network is switched off in code. The same is true of this website.

One honest caveat: our push notification provider's own software collects some device and session information as a normal part of its operation. That is described under "Who else receives your data".

What we collect, and why

Everything below is collected because MonkeyHub needs it to work. There is no optional data collection for our benefit.

Your account

  • Username — required. It is permanent: once chosen it cannot be changed, because it is stamped into @mentions inside other people's messages.
  • Display name — optional, free text. In practice this is often a child's real first name. It appears on every message you send, in the quoted excerpt when someone replies to you, and as the heading of the push notification your recipients see.
  • Email address — optional. You can add one at sign-up or later, or leave it blank. It is used for account recovery and for contacting you about the service. It is deliberately hidden from friend lists and room member lists; only you see it on your own profile.
  • Password — stored as a hash, never as plain text. Please read the "Security" section for an honest description of how strong that hash is.
  • Recovery phrase — stored as a hash. Used only to authorise a password reset.
  • Profile picture — optional. See "Photos, videos, voice notes and documents" below for an important note about how images are served.
  • Privacy preferences — your per-item visibility settings (who can see your avatar, your status, your last-seen time, your read receipts, and who can send you friend requests). Your username and display name are always visible to other users and cannot be hidden.

Messages and their contents

  • The text of every message you send, stored on our server in readable form.
  • Reactions you leave (which emoji, on which message, when).
  • Pinned messages you pin in a room.
  • Replies — when you reply to a message, a snapshot of the original (its author, their display name, and up to 140 characters of its text) is saved onto your reply.
  • @mentions — the user ID and username of each person you mention.
  • Read receipts — the time you last read each room. Your privacy settings control whether other people can see yours (default: nobody). A separate setting on your own device controls whether you see other people's (default: off). The two are independent — hiding yours does not stop you seeing theirs, and showing yours does not reveal theirs to you.
  • Typing indicators — a momentary record that you are typing in a room.
  • Disappearing (view-once) photos — when a view-once photo is opened, we record who opened it and when. The image itself is deleted once everyone has seen it, or after 14 days. The message row itself is not deleted: the fact that you sent a view-once photo, and when, stays in the conversation as an empty bubble.
  • Room settings — a room's name, description, image, member list, join times, whether it is private, and any message-lifetime setting.

Friends and friend requests

  • Who you are friends with, and who you have a pending or refused request with, in both directions, with the times.
  • The free-text greeting attached to a friend request, and any further messages exchanged around that request before it is accepted.
  • For child accounts, which stage of guardian approval a request is at, and which guardian refused it if one did.

Photos, videos, voice notes and documents

Whatever you send: images, videos, recorded voice messages, and documents, along with the file name you gave the document, its size, its type, its dimensions and its duration. These are stored on our own server.

One thing you should know: attachments and profile pictures are served from long, unguessable web addresses that have no expiry and no signature. The address is hard to guess, but it does not stop working, and it is not tied to your login. If one of those addresses is shared, forwarded or leaked, whoever has it can open that file indefinitely. We are working to change this; today, that is how it behaves.

Calls

  • A call record: who called whom, in which room, audio or video, when it started, when it was answered, when it ended, and which of your devices took the call.
  • Connection setup data (technical "signalling" messages). These briefly contain your device's local and public IP addresses — that is how two phones find each other. They are deleted within a few minutes once the other device has picked them up. Records that were never picked up — from a call that was never answered, for example — stay until the call is cleaned up or your account is deleted.
  • The call itself is not recorded and not stored. Audio and video go directly between the participants where possible. When a direct connection is impossible — we estimate somewhere between one call in seven and one in ten, typically behind restrictive network equipment — the media is relayed through our own server. It stays encrypted in transit and we do not record it, but the relay does see both participants' IP addresses.

Your devices and push notifications

For each device you sign in on we store: a random device ID generated on that device, the platform (iOS, Android, web, etc.), push notification tokens, and the device's name. Be aware that on iPhones the device name is very often the owner's real name — "Emma's iPhone". It is shown in the app so you can move a call between your devices.

Sign in with Apple or Google

If you use these, we store the stable, opaque user identifier that Apple or Google gives us, plus the email address they return (which for Apple may be a private relay address). We match accounts on that identifier, never on the email address. For Sign in with Apple we also store a long-lived Apple token, which we use for two things: to tell Apple to revoke the connection when you delete your MonkeyHub account, and to re-check periodically (roughly once a day per account) whether you have revoked MonkeyHub's access from your own Apple ID settings, so that we notice rather than sitting on a dead grant.

Child accounts

If a guardian creates a child account, we store the child's year of birth, a flag marking the account as a child account, the guardian relationship, and the guardian's permission settings for that child (whether they may make calls, send media, request contacts, create rooms). We also store the child's free-text requests to their guardian ("can I join this room, because…") and the guardian's decisions on them. Birth year is stored solely for children's-privacy and age-appropriateness purposes; we do not use it for anything else. See "Children's accounts" below.

Pairing codes

When a guardian sets up a child's device, a six-digit code is generated. We store a hash of the code, not the digits, plus which device redeemed it. That makes the redeemed code a lasting record linking a child's account to a specific handset.

Reports and blocks

If you block someone, we record it. If you report a user or a message, we record who filed it (unless the account is later deleted), what was reported, the reason you chose, and when. Reports are moderation records and are handled differently from everything else on deletion — see "Deleting your account".

Security records

We count failed sign-in and failed recovery-phrase attempts per account, so that an account under attack can be temporarily locked. These records are keyed to the account, not to an IP address: the code that counts failed attempts cannot read your IP. That is not the same as saying no part of our infrastructure sees it. Cloudflare and our web server necessarily handle your IP address in order to route your request, and when a call is relayed our coturn server logs the relay session — and because the relay credential contains your MonkeyHub user ID, those logs associate a user ID with an IP address and a call time.

Presence

Whether you are online, away or offline, and the time you were last active. We keep only the current value; we do not keep a history of your comings and goings.

What MonkeyHub stores on your own device

Some data never leaves your phone but is still worth knowing about, particularly if the device is shared, backed up, or lost.

Kept in the device's secure storage (iOS Keychain / Android encrypted storage):

  • Your session: user ID, username, display name, whether this is a child device.
  • A random device ID, created on first launch.
  • Your app preferences (theme, layout, notification toggles and similar). One of these holds the user ID of the account that last dismissed the recovery-phrase reminder, and preferences are never cleared — so on a shared or handed-on device, that identifier remains after sign-out.

Kept as ordinary, unencrypted files in the app's storage:

  • A copy of your recent conversations — roughly the last 120 messages per room, plus your room list with their message previews, saved as plain text files so the app opens instantly and works offline. This is not encrypted by the app. A full device backup or a forensic extraction of an unlocked phone would expose conversation history in the clear. It is protected by your device's own passcode and disk encryption, and by nothing else.
  • Downloaded voice notes, saved as ordinary audio files for offline playback.
  • Downloaded images, cached for up to 30 days by the standard image cache.
  • A list of attachment web addresses, so the app does not have to look them up again. Because those addresses do not expire, this file is a durable set of direct links to your attachments.

Signing out clears your cached conversations. It does not clear everything. The downloaded voice notes, the image cache, the attachment-address list, your app preferences and the child-device marker all remain. On iPhones and iPads, some Keychain items survive uninstalling the app — that is how Apple's Keychain works. Your session keys, your preferences and the child-device marker are in that group: reinstalling MonkeyHub does not reset the child-device marker on that handset. The random device ID is not — it is cleared when the app is removed, and a new one is generated on reinstall.

Deleting your account does not reach your devices. The server-side purge described below cannot delete files on your phone, or on the phones of people you talked to. If you want the local copies gone, sign out and delete the app, and remember that on iOS a small amount of preference data persists in the Keychain.

If you save a photo to your camera roll, or share a received document out to another app, that copy leaves MonkeyHub entirely and is no longer governed by this policy.

Why we are allowed to process this (legal bases)

For users in the UK, the EU and other places with equivalent law, here is the lawful basis for each purpose. There is not one blanket basis for everything.

  • Running the service — creating your account, signing you in, delivering messages and media, showing presence and read receipts, connecting calls, sending push notifications. Basis: performance of a contract with you (our Terms of Service). Without a username and a password we cannot give you an account at all; without the message data there is no service.
  • Keeping accounts secure — counting failed sign-in attempts, temporarily locking an account under attack, rate limiting. Basis: legitimate interests. Our interest is preventing account takeover, which is also directly in your interest.
  • Safety and moderation — blocks, reports, and acting on them; guardian controls; keeping a moderation record after an account is deleted. Basis: legitimate interests in keeping the service, and particularly its child users, safe from abuse. This interest is what allows us to retain a report after its author leaves; you can object, and we will weigh it, but a service where reporters can erase their own reports by deleting their account is not a safe service.
  • Child accounts — creating and supervising them, storing birth year and guardian permissions. Basis: performance of a contract with the guardian, plus legitimate interests in child safety, and where local law requires it, holder-of-parental-responsibility authorisation. We deliberately do not rely on a child's own consent for anything.
  • Responding to you and to legal obligations — support requests, and any disclosure we are legally compelled to make. Basis: legitimate interests and legal obligation respectively.

We do not rely on consent for any core processing, which means there is no consent to withdraw for it. If you disagree with a legitimate-interests basis, you have the right to object — see "Your rights".

Who else receives your data

MonkeyHub uses very few external services, and none of them are analytics or advertising. But some of them receive real data, and one of them receives more than you might expect.

Where we have a contractual relationship with a recipient — our push provider, and the identity providers we integrate with — that relationship binds them to protect your data and forbids using it for their own advertising on our instructions. Some of the recipients below are not in a contractual relationship with us at all: Google's public STUN servers, and any website a message links to, receive data simply because your device contacts them. We have no agreement with those parties and no control over what they do with what they see. That is a reason to remove those flows, and we intend to.

OneSignal (push notifications) — the largest disclosure

OneSignal, a US company, delivers your push notifications. It receives:

  • The full text of every message you send. Not only when the recipient is away — every time. When you send a text message, MonkeyHub hands its text to OneSignal once for each other member of the room, so it can be shown on their lock screen. There is no condition under which a text message skips this. Because MonkeyHub is not end-to-end encrypted, the notification body is the message. For photos, videos and voice notes OneSignal receives a label like "📷 Image" instead; for documents it receives the file name.
  • The sender's display name, the room ID, the message ID, the sender's ID, and your unread count.
  • Your MonkeyHub user ID, used as a permanent identifier so it knows which devices to wake.
  • For calls, the caller's display name and the type of call. For group calls, the room's name and the name of whoever started the call, sent to everyone invited — and a room name can itself be personal.
  • OneSignal's own software on your device also collects device model, OS version, app version, timezone, language and session counts, as part of its normal operation.
  • On the web version of MonkeyHub, your browser loads OneSignal's own script from OneSignal's servers every time you open the app. That request discloses your IP address and browser details to OneSignal before any notification is involved.

There is currently no way to stop your message text reaching OneSignal while still using MonkeyHub. Turning notifications off in your device settings, or turning them off inside the app, stops the notification being shown — it does not stop the text being sent to OneSignal, because that decision is made on our server before your device is involved. If this is unacceptable to you, MonkeyHub is not the right app for that conversation.

Apple

  • Push delivery (APNs). Every notification to an iPhone or iPad passes through Apple's servers, including the message text described above. For incoming calls, we send Apple a call notification containing the caller's display name, the call type and a link to the caller's avatar.
  • Sign in with Apple, if you use it. Apple learns that your Apple ID signed into MonkeyHub, and when. We exchange tokens with Apple to verify your identity, to re-check the grant periodically, and to revoke the connection when you delete your account.

Google

  • Push delivery (FCM) on Android, including call notifications containing the caller's display name and avatar link.
  • Sign in with Google, if you use it. Google learns that your Google account signed into MonkeyHub, and when.
  • Google's public STUN servers. Every voice or video call begins by asking these servers what your public IP address looks like from the internet. This discloses your IP address and the timing of every call to Google. It carries no name or account identifier. We consider this avoidable and intend to remove it.

Cloudflare

All traffic between your device and our server passes through Cloudflare, which provides our secure connection and protects the server from attack. Because encryption terminates at Cloudflare's edge before continuing to our machine, Cloudflare is technically positioned to see the contents of that traffic, including message text and uploaded files. This is the standard arrangement for any site behind a content delivery network, and it is another reason the end-to-end encryption disclosure above matters.

Oracle Cloud

Our database, our files and our call relay run on virtual machines rented from Oracle Cloud. Oracle is our hosting provider and is technically positioned to access the machines it hosts, in the way any infrastructure provider is.

Websites you or your contacts link to

When a message contains a web link, your device fetches that page directly to build the little preview card, and then loads the preview image from that same website. The request identifies itself to that site as Facebook's link crawler rather than as MonkeyHub, because many sites only serve preview information to known crawlers — which means the site's own logs will record your visit under Facebook's name. Either way the site receives your IP address and the time you viewed the message — not ours. Anyone who sends a link into a room can learn the approximate location and viewing time of everyone in that room, including children. We consider this a genuine weakness and intend to fix it by fetching previews on our server instead. Until then: treat links from people you do not know with the same caution you would in any messenger. Tapping a link opens it in your browser, where that site's own tracking applies as normal.

Our own call relay

When a call cannot connect directly, the audio and video are relayed by our own coturn server. That is our infrastructure, not a third party, but it does see both participants' IP addresses and it logs the session.

App stores

Apple and Google collect their own information about your download and device when you install MonkeyHub from their stores. That happens outside the app and is governed by their policies, not this one.

Law enforcement and legal process

We will disclose data if we are legally compelled to. We will tell you when we are permitted to.

Where your data is stored and sent

MonkeyHub's database and files run on a virtual machine located in Sweden, rented from Oracle Cloud and administered by the developer. Our call relay runs on a second rented machine. The operator is located in Türkiye, which means the person administering the service accesses data held in the European Economic Area from outside it.

Some of the recipients named above are located outside your country, and outside the UK and the European Economic Area — in particular OneSignal, Apple, Google, Cloudflare and Oracle are US companies with global infrastructure. Where personal data is transferred internationally, we rely on the transfer safeguards those providers put in place, which include the EU Standard Contractual Clauses and, where applicable, certification under the EU–US and UK–US Data Privacy Framework. You can ask us for details of the safeguards for any specific recipient at info@walhallaa.com.

Because push notifications, sign-in and call setup work by contacting servers around the world, using MonkeyHub necessarily involves some international data transfer. There is no way to use the app's notification or calling features without it.

How long we keep things

  • Messages, photos, videos, voice notes and documents — kept until you delete them, until the room is deleted or emptied, until your account is deleted, or until the message's lifetime expires if it was sent in a room with a message lifetime set. In an ordinary room with no lifetime, messages are kept indefinitely. Note that deleting a room's last member wipes that room and its media entirely.
  • View-once photos — the image is deleted as soon as every recipient has opened it, or after 14 days, whichever comes first. The record that a view-once photo was sent remains in the conversation.
  • Attachments no message points to any more — automatically cleaned up, with anything under 24 hours old left alone.
  • Call records — completed, missed and rejected calls are deleted about a day after they end. Call connection data containing IP addresses is deleted within a few minutes once it has been picked up.
  • Call relay logs — when a call has to be relayed through our own server, that server records the session, and because the relay credential contains your MonkeyHub user ID those log lines associate a user ID with an IP address and a time. They are rotated by the operating system's standard log rotation on that machine.
  • Push notification delivery records — deleted every few hours, once the notification can no longer be recalled.
  • Pairing codes — expire automatically. Redeemed codes stay attached to the child's account until the account is deleted.
  • Failed sign-in records — deleted automatically once the account has had no failed attempts for a day.
  • Presence — we store only your current status and last-seen time, overwritten each time; there is no history.
  • Reports — kept for 24 months from the date the report was filed, then deleted automatically by a scheduled job. This applies whether the report was acted on or not.
  • Your account and profile — kept until you delete the account.

Deleting your account: what goes, and what stays

How to delete your account

  • In the app: open your profile, then Delete Account. Deletion is immediate and permanent. There is no waiting period and no way to undo it.
  • On the web: if you have already uninstalled MonkeyHub, use the account deletion page, or email info@walhallaa.com from the address on your account. We will verify that the request is genuinely yours before acting on it, and we aim to complete it within 30 days.
  • Child accounts: a child cannot delete their own account from their device. Their guardian deletes it from the Family screen in the guardian's app, which runs exactly the same erasure. A guardian can also request it via the web link above. If you are a child and your guardian will not delete your account, write to us and we will.

If you signed in with Apple, deleting your account also tells Apple to revoke MonkeyHub's access to your Apple ID, so the connection is properly severed on Apple's side too.

What deletion removes

Everything that identifies you as the owner of it:

  • Your user record: username, display name, email, password hash, recovery-phrase hash, birth year, account type, guardian permission settings, privacy preferences, presence.
  • Your profile picture file.
  • Every message you sent, together with its attachments, its reactions, its pins and its view-once open records. (An attachment that someone forwarded into another room survives there, because it is now part of their message.)
  • Reactions and pins you left on other people's messages, and records of view-once photos you opened.
  • Your room memberships. Any room left empty by your departure is deleted completely, including its media. In a room that survives, ownership passes to the longest-standing remaining member.
  • Your friendships and friend requests, in both directions, and the messages you sent around them.
  • Blocks you made and blocks made against you.
  • Guardian relationships, pairing codes, release codes and approval requests.
  • Your one-to-one call records and call connection data, in both directions.
  • Your registered devices and push tokens.
  • Your Sign in with Apple and Sign in with Google connections.
  • Typing indicators and pending notification records addressed to you.

What deletion does not remove

We would rather tell you this than let you find out:

  • Reports you filed are kept for the 24-month retention period. We remove your identity from them — your user ID is erased and the report becomes anonymous — but the reason, what you reported and when are retained. This is deliberate: a service where anyone can erase their own report trail by deleting their account cannot protect its users.
  • Reports filed about you are kept in full for the same period, including the identifier of the account that was reported.
  • Group call records are currently not deleted at all. Rows recording that you started or joined a group call, which device you joined from, and when you joined and left are left behind — as are unconsumed connection-setup records, which can contain your device's IP address. This is a defect in our deletion code, not a policy decision, and we are fixing it. If you want these removed now, email us and we will remove them by hand.
  • Failed sign-in records tied to your account are aged out by a scheduled job rather than deleted at once. The job runs every six hours and only clears records idle for a day, so they can outlast your account by up to about thirty hours.
  • @mentions of you inside other people's messages keep your user ID and username, because they are part of someone else's message.
  • Replies to your messages keep your display name and up to 140 characters of what you wrote, for the same reason.
  • A room's last-message preview may still show a snippet of your final message on that room's row.
  • The other side of a friend request. If someone wrote to you while a friend request was pending, their messages in that exchange are not removed by your deletion.
  • Delivery records for other people. Records that a notification for one of your messages was sent to someone else remain until they age out on their own schedule.
  • Disappearing-photo stubs. When a view-once photo has been opened or has expired, the image is deleted but the message row stays.
  • Our push provider's records. We do not currently call OneSignal to erase your identifier and notification history there when you delete your account. Their own retention schedule applies. If you want this done, email us and we will request it manually. We intend to automate this.
  • Everything already on a device. Deletion runs on our server. It cannot reach the copies on your phone, or on the phones of people you spoke with.
  • Anything the law requires us to keep, including material preserved for a child-safety report.

If any of this is unacceptable to you, contact info@walhallaa.com and we will do what we can by hand.

Children's accounts and the guardian model

MonkeyHub is designed for families, and it has a specific child account type.

How a child account is created. Only an adult can create one, from inside their own signed-in MonkeyHub account. The guardian chooses the child's username and display name, provides the child's year of birth, and sets what the child is allowed to do. The child's device is then paired using a six-digit code the guardian generates. A child account cannot be created by a child, cannot be signed into with a password, and cannot be created from a fresh install without a guardian.

Guardian authorisation. By creating a child account, the guardian authorises the collection described in this policy on the child's behalf. We do not currently show a separate consent screen before the account is created, and we do not store a separate record of consent beyond the guardianship itself. Guardians should read this policy in full before creating an account for a child — in particular the sections on end-to-end encryption and on push notifications.

What we collect about a child is the same as for any other user — username, display name, messages, photos, voice notes, call records, device information and push tokens — plus the child-specific items: year of birth, the guardian relationship, the guardian's permission settings, and the child's free-text requests to their guardian.

We collect a child's year of birth solely to identify the account as a child's and apply the right protections. We do not use it for anything else. We do not collect a child's precise date of birth, address, phone number, school, or location.

Public rooms are off for children, permanently. Discover — the directory of rooms belonging to people you have not met — is not reachable from a child account, and there is no setting that turns it on. This is not a guardian decision and cannot be overridden. A child can still take part in any room they are invited to, which is how a child joins a room with friends they already know.

What guardians can do. A guardian can, at any time: change what the child is allowed to do, approve or refuse the child's contact and room requests, revoke a contact, revoke a paired device, review the personal information held about the child by contacting us, and delete the child's account entirely — which runs the full erasure described above.

Security: what protects your data, and what does not

What is true:

  • All traffic between the app and our servers uses TLS (HTTPS and secure websockets).
  • The server is a machine the developer administers directly, with no third-party chat platform in the middle.
  • Failed sign-in and failed recovery attempts are counted, and an account under attack is temporarily locked.
  • Six-digit pairing codes and room passwords are stored as an unsalted SHA-256 hash rather than in readable form.   • Call media is encrypted between participants by WebRTC and is never recorded.

What is also true, and which we would rather state than let you assume otherwise:

  • Messages are not end-to-end encrypted. The operator can read them. This is the most important sentence in this policy.
  • Password hashing is weak by modern standards. Your password is hashed on your device with a single pass of SHA-256, without a salt, and that hash is what we store and compare. There is no bcrypt, scrypt or Argon2, and no key stretching. In practice this means two things: a copy of our database would let an attacker sign in as any user without needing to crack anything, and common passwords would be recoverable from it easily. Please use a long, unique password on MonkeyHub, and do not reuse a password you use anywhere else. We intend to move to a proper password hashing scheme.
  • There are no server-side sessions. Most operations in MonkeyHub identify you by a user ID your app sends with the request, rather than by a session token the server issued and can revoke. This is a known weakness we intend to fix. Setting or changing a recovery phrase, and linking a sign-in provider, are deliberately password-gated because they would otherwise be complete account-takeover routes; other operations do not have that gate.
  • Attachment links do not expire. Files are served from long, unguessable but permanent web addresses that are not tied to your session. Anyone who obtains such an address keeps access to that file.
  • Cached conversations on your device are not encrypted by the app — they rely on your device's own passcode and disk encryption.
  • TLS terminates before our machine. Cloudflare decrypts your traffic at its edge in order to route and protect it. That is normal for any site behind a network provider, and it means your traffic is readable at that point.

No service can promise perfect security, and a service with the weaknesses listed above certainly cannot. We are telling you where the soft spots are so that you can make an informed decision about what you send.
