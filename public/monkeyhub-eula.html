6. Child accounts and guardian controls

MonkeyHub supports supervised child accounts. A guardian creates a child account from their own account, and a child signs in on a device using a six-digit pairing code rather than a password.

Guardians control what a child may do without asking. The guardian sets four permissions — calls, sending photos/video/voice, requesting new contacts, and creating rooms — and a child can never edit them. Where a permission is off, the child can send a request to their guardian, who approves or denies it.

Public rooms are not a permission. Discover — the directory of rooms belonging to people the child has not met — is permanently unavailable to child accounts and cannot be enabled by anyone, including a guardian. A child can still take part in any room they are invited to.

Because of that, the following are breaches of this agreement, and we treat them as serious:

  • Attempting to bypass, disable, spoof or otherwise circumvent guardian permissions, approval requests, pairing or release codes, or the guardian relationship itself.
  • Helping a child circumvent their guardian's controls, or coaching them on how to.
  • Creating an adult account for a child in order to escape supervision, or misstating a child's birth year.
  • Using an adult account to solicit personal information from a child, or to contact a child who has not been approved as a contact.

Guardians should also understand these limits honestly:

  • What we store about a child. A child's account holds a username, a display name, the permissions you set, the guardian relationship, and — if you supply one — the child's year of birth. We store the birth year only to work out which children's-privacy rules apply to the account. We do not verify it, and we do not use it for anything else.
  • A signed-in child cannot delete their own account. The app refuses it. Only a guardian can delete a child's account, from the guardian's own device. If you are a child and want your account deleted, ask your guardian, or write to info@walhallaa.com and we will do it.
  • Guardian controls are not a content filter and not surveillance of your conversations. They govern what a child can do in the app. Guardians cannot read a child's messages or rooms, and nothing screens or automatically moderates what anyone says. There is one exception, and it is deliberate: when a child asks to add a contact or to join a room, the guardian who has to approve it sees the child's request — including the free-text note attached to it, and the free-text greeting a stranger attached to a request to add the child. Supervision is a set of permissions, not a guarantee of safety.
  • Revoking a contact is destructive. Removing a contact from a child's account permanently deletes the entire direct-message conversation between them, for both the child and the other person. It cannot be undone, and the other person is not asked.

Guardians are responsible for their child's use of MonkeyHub under this agreement, and for supervising it.

9. What we do not promise about security

This section is here because a licence agreement that quietly implied more security than the app has would be misleading. Stated plainly:

  • Messages are not end-to-end encrypted. They are stored in our database in readable form on infrastructure the developer controls, and the developer can read them.
  • Message text leaves our servers to deliver notifications. When we send a push notification about a message, the notification body is the message text itself, and it is handed to our push notification provider along with the sender's name and the room it belongs to, so the recipient's device can show it on the lock screen. This happens for every text message, not only when the recipient is away, and there is no setting that prevents it.
  • The 12-word recovery phrase is not an encryption key. It derives nothing and encrypts nothing; it authorises exactly one action, resetting your password.
  • Password protection is weak by modern standards. Passwords are hashed on your device with plain, unsalted SHA-256, and that digest is what is stored and compared. Do not reuse a password you use anywhere else.
  • Attachment links are permanent and unguessable rather than access-controlled. Photos, videos, voice notes and documents are served from long random addresses with no expiry and no signature. That address is effectively the file: anyone who obtains one keeps access to it for as long as it exists on our servers, whether or not they are still in the room.
  • View-once and message-lifetime features are not guarantees. They delete the file on our side, and the record that a view-once photo was sent remains. They cannot stop a recipient photographing their screen with another device. On Android we ask the operating system to block screenshots and screen recording while a disappearing item is on screen; on iOS the operating system offers no equivalent, so an in-device screenshot is not blocked there.
  • Content you save leaves the app. Anything saved to your photo library, shared to another app, or exported is outside our control from that moment.

Connections to our servers use TLS. That protects data from other people on the network. It does not make the content private from us, and it does not make it private from our hosting and network providers: TLS is terminated at our content-delivery provider's edge before the request reaches our own server, so that provider is technically positioned to see the same content we can. The Privacy Policy describes in full what is collected, where it goes, who else receives it, and what survives account deletion. Read it — it is the document that matters most here.

12. Updates, including mandatory updates

MonkeyHub checks once per cold start whether your build is behind the latest one published for your distribution channel. There are two outcomes:

  • An optional update. You are shown an "UPDATE AVAILABLE" sheet with a LATER button. You can dismiss it and carry on. It will ask again on the next launch.
  • A mandatory update. If your version is older than the minimum we still support, the sheet says "UPDATE REQUIRED", has no LATER button, cannot be swiped away, and blocks the Android back button and the desktop Escape key. You cannot use MonkeyHub until you update. We use this only when an old build can no longer talk to the backend safely or correctly.

You agree that we may require mandatory updates on this basis. The destination the update button opens is published by us on the server and can be changed for builds already installed — this is how an app shipped before a store listing existed can still be pointed at the right place.

On platforms with an app store, updates are delivered by that store according to its own settings. On direct-download builds, the update button sends you to our download page. We are not obliged to keep supporting any particular version, platform, or operating system release, and support for older ones will end over time.
